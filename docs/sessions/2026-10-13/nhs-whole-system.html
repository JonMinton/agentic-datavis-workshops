NotePreparation notes, not a session writeup

These notes revisit the 2026-10-09 preparation page and take its idea a step further. Nothing here is a decision by a group. The design decisions below are drafting decisions, made by the agent at the facilitator’s request, and every one is open for a group to overturn. Drafted by Claude Opus 5.5 from Public Health Scotland open data.

The question

Patient flow is a whole-system problem. Whether someone waits in A&E depends partly on whether a ward bed is free, and that depends partly on whether the people in those beds can be discharged. Seeing this needs charts that put measures from different parts of the system side by side. But the data arrives in silos, published by different teams, and the habit is to analyse each silo on its own. Technique isn’t the obstacle: putting two datasets on one plot is easy. The obstacle is knowing the parts depend on each other, and having code that makes that dependence visible.

The earlier page had already put two silos on one chart, so the question for this page is narrower: does the code that draws the chart carry the whole-system idea, or only the chart?

What the earlier page got right, and where it stopped short

The 2026-10-09 page made the case for crossing silos with Public Health Scotland data: A&E four-hour performance against beds occupied by delayed discharges, and the “exit block” story that links them. Two of its choices work against that aim.

  1. It rejected the $ approach. It showed aes(x = delays_scot$delayed_beds, y = ed_scot$pct_4h) as the fragile option, because ggplot2 pairs the two vectors by position, not by month. That’s true, and it’s still the most readable expression of “this silo against that one”. The page gave up the readability to get the safety.
  2. Its join erased the silos. The recommended route was inner_join() into a table called both_scot. That’s correct, but after it, nothing in the plotting code says that the columns came from different parts of the system. The analyst writing the next chart starts again from one table, which is the silo habit again.

It also named the data frames after the publishing teams (ed, delays), not after where they sit in the system, and it stopped at two silos, jumping from the front door straight to the back.

Design decisions

Each decision below is recorded as considered, chosen and why, for a group to reopen.

1. Name each silo by its place on the patient’s journey. Considered: team names (ed, delays, beds); dataset names (ae_monthly); journey names. Chosen: front_door (A&E), ward (acute beds) and back_door (delayed discharges). The names cost nothing and say what the chart needs said: these are three points on one path, not three reports. Anyone reading flow$back_door$delayed_beds is reminded where the patient is.

2. Keep the silos separate, but align them before plotting. Considered: a joined wide table (the earlier page); layers with their own data (works for time series, can’t make a scatter); $ vectors as they come (fragile). Chosen: a small helper, align_silos(), that takes named silos and the shared keys, refuses any silo with more than one row per key, keeps only keys every silo has, and returns the silos as a named list, each sorted the same way. The silos stay distinct objects with distinct names, and the pairing rule is applied once, in one place, where it can be checked.

3. Use $ freely after alignment. Chosen: plotting code like aes(x = flow$back_door$delayed_beds, y = flow$front_door$pct_4h). Every aesthetic names the silo it came from. Alignment makes it safe, because row i of every silo is the same month.

4. Make the silo itself a variable, so the grammar can map it. Considered: one chart per silo stacked with patchwork (the earlier “silo view”); dual y axes (rejected: two scales on one axis invite the reader to compare lines whose heights mean nothing to each other). Chosen: bind the silos into one long table with bind_rows(..., .id = "stage") and facet by stage, in journey order. “Which part of the system” becomes a column, which is the grammar’s way of saying it matters.

5. Add the middle of the journey. The ward silo is acute bed occupancy from the Public Health Scotland quarterly beds publication. It exists only by quarter, so the other two silos are summed to quarters for the whole-system figures, and a quarter counts only when all three of its months are present. Considered: keep everything monthly and leave the ward out; interpolate occupancy to months (rejected: it invents data). The cost is a shorter, coarser record: 21 quarters, Oct–Dec 2020 to Oct–Dec 2025, starting late in the first pandemic year.

6. Leave admitted patients and ambulances for later. The four-hour figure counts every Type 1 attendance, including the majority who go home and never need a ward bed. Admitted-only figures, and ambulance handover times at the very front door, would sharpen the chain. Neither is in the open files used here.

Sources and joins

Three publications, three silos, and three alignments, one for each figure. Nothing is joined into a single wide table: each align_silos() step trims the silos to the keys they share and hands them back side by side, still named.

Two things the diagram makes visible that the code alone doesn’t. The ward only exists by quarter, so it forces the other two silos up to its grain before they can meet it; and each figure has its own alignment, so the monthly chart can use all 122 months, while the three-silo charts are limited to the 21 quarters the ward covers.

How alignment works, and what it catches

Code 
flow_m <- align_silos(
  front_door = front_door_scot_m,   # A&E, Scotland, by month
  back_door  = back_door_scot_m,    # delayed discharges, Scotland, by month
  by = "month"
)

For the monthly Scotland system, alignment keeps 122 months, July 2016 to August 2026, and drops 108 A&E months that have no delayed-discharge record (that series starts later). The drop is reported, not silent.

If a silo is ambiguous, for example because a month appears twice after a careless append, alignment stops instead of drawing a plausible wrong chart:

Error: silo 'back_door' has 1 key(s) with more than one row; aggregate it to
one row per month first

Candidate mappings (the group chooses)

Candidates (the group chooses) - nhs-whole-system:
  silos:          front_door (A&E), ward (acute beds), back_door (delayed discharges)
  grain:          board x month (two silos) or board x quarter (all three)
  position x/y:   any silo's measure against any other's, after align_silos()
  facet:          stage (journey order), or board
  colour/alpha:   time; or a third silo's measure (e.g. delayed beds on the ward plot)
  silo as data:   bind_rows(.id = "stage") turns "which part of the system" into a variable

Questions for the group:

  • Is the $ version clearer, or just shorter? The test is whether someone new to the data can say, from the code alone, which parts of the system the chart connects.
  • Journey names or team names? front_door is vivid but informal. Some teams would rather see the publication name.
  • Should the helper be visible? It could live in a shared script so the session sees only the $ plots, or be written live as part of the lesson.
  • What belongs between the ward and the back door? Length of stay and discharges by day of the week are both published and both sit on the same path.

What the data can’t say

These are aggregate totals by board and month or quarter. They can’t follow a patient, so they can’t show that the person waiting in A&E was waiting for a bed held by a delayed discharge. The three measures share drivers, including an ageing population, winter, staffing and the pandemic, that could move them together without any causal chain. The four-hour figure includes patients who were never admitted. Acute occupancy is an average over a quarter and a whole board, so it hides the days and hospitals that were full. The three-silo record is only 21 quarters, starting late in the first pandemic year, which is too short to separate a trend from a relationship. Bed definitions and delay definitions are set nationally and can change; a change in definition would look like a change in the system.

It runs every step on this page from start to finish: it fetches the data (or uses the cached copies in data/), builds the three silos, defines align_silos(), sums to quarters, does the three alignments, shows the duplicated-month check, saves the three figures to figures/, and prints the numbers quoted above. Run it from the repository root, or from any empty folder:

It needs only the tidyverse. It was checked on 2026-10-13 by running it in an empty folder, where it downloaded all three files and reproduced every number on this page.

The data comes from the Public Health Scotland open data portal, under the Open Government Licence v3:

  • phs-ae-monthly.csv: Monthly A&E activity and waiting times.
  • phs-delayed-discharge.csv: Delayed discharges in NHSScotland.
  • phs-beds-quarterly.csv: NHS beds information in Scotland, beds by board of treatment and specialty. This page uses the All Acute rows at board level (where Location equals HB). Pulled on 2026-10-13, it runs from October–December 2020 to October–December 2025, and quarters are calendar quarters.

Some download links carry the release month, so they go out of date. If a download fails, take the current link from the dataset page and update the sources table at the top of the script. The page itself also reads phs-health-boards.csv (health board codes and labels), which the script doesn’t need.

```{r}
#| label: silos
#| include: false
# Each silo is built from its own publication and keeps its own name.
# The names describe where the measure sits on a patient's journey, not which team
# publishes it: front door (A&E), ward (acute beds), back door (delayed discharges).

# Front door: Type 1 emergency departments, all attendances, board x month
front_door_m <- ae_raw |>
  filter(DepartmentType == "Type 1", AttendanceCategory == "All") |>
  group_by(board_code = HBT, month = yyyymm(Month)) |>
  summarise(attendances = sum(NumberOfAttendancesAll),
            within_4h   = sum(NumberWithin4HoursAll), .groups = "drop")

# Back door: adults delayed in hospital, all reasons, board x month (Scotland row included)
back_door_m <- dd_raw |>
  filter(AgeGroup == "18plus", ReasonForDelay == "All Delay Reasons") |>
  transmute(board_code = HBT, month = yyyymm(MonthOfDelay),
            bed_days = NumberOfDelayedBedDays,
            delayed_beds = AverageDailyNumberOfDelayedBeds)

# Ward: acute specialties, board-level rows, board x quarter
ward_q <- beds_raw |>
  filter(SpecialtyName == "All Acute", Location == HB) |>
  transmute(board_code = HB, quarter = yq(Quarter),
            occupied_beds = AverageOccupiedBeds,
            occupancy = PercentageOccupancy)
```

```{r}
#| label: align
#| include: false
# The one helper this page adds. It keeps every silo as its own data frame, but trims
# them all to the same keys in the same order, and refuses anything ambiguous. After it
# has run, silo$column vectors from different silos line up row for row.
align_silos <- function(..., by) {
  silos <- list(...)
  stopifnot("give each silo a name" = !is.null(names(silos)) && all(names(silos) != ""))
  for (nm in names(silos)) {
    dupes <- silos[[nm]] |> count(across(all_of(by))) |> filter(n > 1)
    if (nrow(dupes) > 0) {
      stop("silo '", nm, "' has ", nrow(dupes), " key(s) with more than one row; ",
           "aggregate it to one row per ", paste(by, collapse = " x "), " first",
           call. = FALSE)
    }
  }
  keys <- silos |>
    map(\(s) distinct(s, across(all_of(by)))) |>
    reduce(inner_join, by = by) |>
    arrange(across(all_of(by)))
  aligned <- map(silos, \(s) left_join(keys, s, by = by))
  attr(aligned, "dropped") <- map_int(silos, nrow) - nrow(keys)
  aligned
}

# Monthly system, Scotland: front door and back door
front_door_scot_m <- front_door_m |>
  group_by(month) |>
  summarise(attendances = sum(attendances), within_4h = sum(within_4h)) |>
  mutate(pct_4h = 100 * within_4h / attendances)
back_door_scot_m <- back_door_m |> filter(board_code == scotland) |> select(-board_code)

flow_m <- align_silos(front_door = front_door_scot_m, back_door = back_door_scot_m,
                      by = "month")

# Quarterly system: the ward silo only exists by quarter, so the other two are summed
# up to quarters. A quarter counts only if all three of its months are present.
to_quarter <- function(d, ...) {
  d |>
    mutate(quarter = floor_date(month, "quarter")) |>
    group_by(quarter, ...) |>
    filter(n() == 3) |>
    summarise(across(any_of(c("attendances", "within_4h", "bed_days")), sum),
              days = sum(days_in_month(month)), .groups = "drop")
}

front_door_q <- to_quarter(front_door_m, board_code) |>
  mutate(pct_4h = 100 * within_4h / attendances) |> select(-days)
back_door_q  <- to_quarter(back_door_m, board_code) |>
  mutate(delayed_beds = bed_days / days) |> select(-days)

front_door_scot_q <- front_door_q |>
  group_by(quarter) |>
  summarise(attendances = sum(attendances), within_4h = sum(within_4h)) |>
  mutate(pct_4h = 100 * within_4h / attendances)

flow_q <- align_silos(
  front_door = front_door_scot_q,
  ward       = ward_q |> filter(board_code == scotland) |> select(-board_code),
  back_door  = back_door_q |> filter(board_code == scotland) |> select(-board_code),
  by = "quarter"
)

flow_hb_q <- align_silos(
  front_door = front_door_q,
  ward       = ward_q |> filter(board_code != scotland),
  back_door  = back_door_q |> filter(board_code != scotland),
  by = c("board_code", "quarter")
)

n_m   <- nrow(flow_m$front_door)
n_q   <- nrow(flow_q$ward)
q1    <- min(flow_q$ward$quarter)
qn    <- max(flow_q$ward$quarter)
n_hb  <- n_distinct(flow_hb_q$ward$board_code)
dropped_m <- attr(flow_m, "dropped")

# The misaligned case the helper exists to catch: one silo has a month twice
caught <- tryCatch(
  align_silos(front_door = front_door_scot_m,
              back_door  = bind_rows(back_door_scot_m, slice(back_door_scot_m, 1)),
              by = "month"),
  error = conditionMessage
)
```

Two things the diagram makes visible that the code alone doesn't. The ward only exists by quarter, so it forces the other two silos up to its grain before they can meet it; and each figure has its own alignment, so the monthly chart can use all `r n_m` months, while the three-silo charts are limited to the `r n_q` quarters the ward covers.

## How alignment works, and what it catches

```{r}
#| label: show-helper
#| echo: true
#| eval: false
flow_m <- align_silos(
  front_door = front_door_scot_m,   # A&E, Scotland, by month
  back_door  = back_door_scot_m,    # delayed discharges, Scotland, by month
  by = "month"
)
```

For the monthly Scotland system, alignment keeps `r n_m` months, `r fmt_m(min(flow_m$front_door$month))` to `r fmt_m(max(flow_m$front_door$month))`, and drops `r dropped_m[["front_door"]]` A&E months that have no delayed-discharge record (that series starts later). The drop is reported, not silent.

If a silo is ambiguous, for example because a month appears twice after a careless append, alignment stops instead of drawing a plausible wrong chart:

```{r}
#| label: show-caught
#| echo: false
cat(strwrap(paste("Error:", caught), width = 80), sep = "\n")
```

## Candidate mappings (the group chooses)

``` text
Candidates (the group chooses) - nhs-whole-system:
  silos:          front_door (A&E), ward (acute beds), back_door (delayed discharges)
  grain:          board x month (two silos) or board x quarter (all three)
  position x/y:   any silo's measure against any other's, after align_silos()
  facet:          stage (journey order), or board
  colour/alpha:   time; or a third silo's measure (e.g. delayed beds on the ward plot)
  silo as data:   bind_rows(.id = "stage") turns "which part of the system" into a variable
```

Questions for the group:

- **Is the `$` version clearer, or just shorter?** The test is whether someone new to the data can say, from the code alone, which parts of the system the chart connects.
- **Journey names or team names?** `front_door` is vivid but informal. Some teams would rather see the publication name.
- **Should the helper be visible?** It could live in a shared script so the session sees only the `$` plots, or be written live as part of the lesson.
- **What belongs between the ward and the back door?** Length of stay and discharges by day of the week are both published and both sit on the same path.

## What the data can't say

These are aggregate totals by board and month or quarter. They can't follow a patient, so they can't show that the person waiting in A&E was waiting for a bed held by a delayed discharge. The three measures share drivers, including an ageing population, winter, staffing and the pandemic, that could move them together without any causal chain. The four-hour figure includes patients who were never admitted. Acute occupancy is an average over a quarter and a whole board, so it hides the days and hospitals that were full. The three-silo record is only `r n_q` quarters, starting late in the first pandemic year, which is too short to separate a trend from a relationship. Bed definitions and delay definitions are set nationally and can change; a change in definition would look like a change in the system.

## Reproducing this page: download the whole pipeline as one R script

**[Download `whole-system.R`](whole-system.R){download="whole-system.R"}** (annotated, about 300 lines). It runs every step on this page from start to finish: it fetches the data (or uses the cached copies in `data/`), builds the three silos, defines `align_silos()`, sums to quarters, does the three alignments, shows the duplicated-month check, saves the three figures to `figures/`, and prints the numbers quoted above. Run it from the repository root, or from any empty folder:

``` sh
Rscript whole-system.R
```

It needs only the tidyverse. It was checked on 2026-10-13 by running it in an empty folder, where it downloaded all three files and reproduced every number on this page.

The data comes from the [Public Health Scotland open data portal](https://www.opendata.nhs.scot/), under the [Open Government Licence v3](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/doc/open-government-licence/version/3/):

- `phs-ae-monthly.csv`: [Monthly A&E activity and waiting times](https://www.opendata.nhs.scot/dataset/monthly-accident-and-emergency-activity-and-waiting-times).
- `phs-delayed-discharge.csv`: [Delayed discharges in NHSScotland](https://www.opendata.nhs.scot/dataset/delayed-discharges-in-nhsscotland).
- `phs-beds-quarterly.csv`: [NHS beds information in Scotland](https://www.opendata.nhs.scot/dataset/hospital-beds-information), beds by board of treatment and specialty. This page uses the `All Acute` rows at board level (where `Location` equals `HB`). Pulled on 2026-10-13, it runs from October–December 2020 to October–December 2025, and quarters are calendar quarters.

Some download links carry the release month, so they go out of date. If a download fails, take the current link from the dataset page and update the `sources` table at the top of the script. The page itself also reads `phs-health-boards.csv` ([health board codes and labels](https://www.opendata.nhs.scot/dataset/geography-codes-and-labels)), which the script doesn't need.

Cite Public Health Scotland as the source.
